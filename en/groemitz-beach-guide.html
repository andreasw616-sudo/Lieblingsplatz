Grömitz beach: every section, beach chair and access point explained

In short

Grömitz has an 8 km south-facing sandy beach divided into roughly 21 sections that are marked by symbols rather than numbers – from “Seestern” (starfish) to “Maus” (mouse). Lifeguards patrol in high season. Beach access is covered by the ostseecard tourist tax: €4.00 per day in high season, €2.50 in low season.

How long is the beach in Grömitz?

The bathing beach at Grömitz runs for about 8 kilometres, from Lensterstrand in the north to the southern edge of the resort. It sits on the Bay of Lübeck and therefore faces south – which is why it is comparatively sheltered and catches sun all day long. The sand is fine and the water shelves gently.

That southern aspect is the key difference from many other Baltic resorts. Where beaches face north-east, the prevailing westerly wind pushes noticeably more swell and colder water ashore. In Grömitz the sea stays calm on most summer days.

The beach sections and their symbols

Grömitz does not number its beach sections – it marks them with symbols. Each section has its own sign, repeated on the beach chairs. It is a genuinely useful system: “meet me at the seahorse” is far easier to remember than a number.

Worth knowingThe exact allocation of symbols can shift between seasons when beach chair operators swap areas. The signs at the beach entrance are always authoritative.

Which section suits whom?

Families with small children do well between Seepferdchen and Sonne: short walks to toilets, playgrounds and food, shallow water and lifeguards in sight.

If you want quiet, head south towards Delfin, Möwe, Fisch and Maus, or go right up north. Ten minutes on foot is usually enough to leave the crowds behind, even in August.

With a dog, use the marked dog beach in the north near the Klinik Ostseedeich, which has its own car park. Please note that pets are not permitted in our apartment.

Naturism is possible on a separate, signposted section. Clothed and nudist beaches are clearly divided.

Renting a beach chair

On the Baltic, the wicker Strandkorb is not a luxury – it is windbreak, parasol and locker in one. Several operators rent them along the Grömitz sections, including through strandbutler.de and strandidyll.de. Prices and availability vary by operator, section and season.

  • Book ahead online during the German school holidays and long weekends – the good rows go early.
  • Take the weekly rate rather than daily if you are staying more than three days; it is usually far cheaper.
  • The front row is not always best. Further back you get more shelter and longer evening sun, because the dune edge does not cast shade on you.

What does beach access cost?

Access to the licensed bathing beach is covered by the ostseecard, the local visitor tax. In 2026 it costs €4.00 per person per day in high season and €2.50 in low season for Grömitz and Lensterstrand. We issue the card on arrival and it is charged per night.

Besides beach access it includes reduced bus fares along the coast, cheaper entry to the Grömitzer Welle pool, free guided walks and sports sessions, and discounts at bike hire shops. Full detail in our article on the ostseecard and visitor tax.

Lifeguards, water quality and swimming with children

In high season the bathing beach is patrolled by the DLRG lifeguard service, and water quality in the Bay of Lübeck is monitored regularly and consistently good. The shallow entry makes swimming with children relaxed – but the usual Baltic caveats apply: onshore wind can build a short, steep chop, and an offshore easterly can cool the surface water noticeably within a day.

Watch the flags on the lifeguard towers: red means no swimming, red-and-yellow marks the patrolled zone, yellow means extra care.

200 metres from our apartment to the sand

Our apartment at Seestraße 9a is about 200 metres from the beach – under three minutes on foot, with no busy road to cross. Close enough that going down for two hours in the morning and coming back for lunch actually happens. More on the beachside apartment page.

If you would rather look at the sea from above: the 27 m² roof terrace is the place for sunset over the Bay of Lübeck.

Seven practical tips

  1. Early or late. July and August are busiest between 11am and 3pm. Before 10am and after 4pm you get far more space.
  2. Check the wind direction. Grömitz is better sheltered than resorts further east in a north-westerly – and the reverse in a south-easterly.
  3. Do not underestimate the sun. Reflection off water and pale sand raises UV exposure considerably.
  4. Water shoes for children. There are shell fragments in the shallows in places.
  5. Jellyfish. A persistent easterly in late summer can bring them in. The pale moon jellyfish is harmless; the red-brown lion's mane stings.
  6. Look before you book a beach chair. One walk along the section shows where shelter and view are best.
  7. Sunset from the pier. The 398-metre Seebrücke is the finest spot in town in the evening – and it is free.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the beach in Grömitz?

The bathing beach at Grömitz is around 8 kilometres long, fine-sanded and south-facing, running from Lensterstrand in the north to the southern edge of the resort.

What does the beach in Grömitz cost?

Beach access is covered by the ostseecard visitor tax: €4.00 per person per day in high season and €2.50 in low season for Grömitz and Lensterstrand in 2026. There is no separate beach entry fee.

Is there a dog beach in Grömitz?

Yes, there is a marked dog beach in the north near the Klinik Ostseedeich, with its own car park. Please note that pets are not permitted in the Lieblingsplatz Grömitz apartment.

What are the beach sections in Grömitz called?

Grömitz marks its sections with symbols rather than numbers – for example Seestern (starfish), Seepferdchen (seahorse), Sonne (sun), Leuchtturm (lighthouse), Herz (heart), Delfin (dolphin) and Möwe (gull). Signs at each beach entrance show the symbol.

Is the beach in Grömitz supervised?

In high season the licensed bathing beach is patrolled by DLRG lifeguards. Watch the flags on the towers: red means no swimming, red-and-yellow marks the patrolled zone.

Where can I rent a beach chair in Grömitz?

Several operators rent along the beach sections and can be booked online through strandbutler.de and strandidyll.de, among others. Book ahead during German school holidays; weekly rates are usually better value than daily ones.
