Grömitz pier and diving gondola: length, prices and the best time to go

Leon Kreutzheide · Updated 23 August 2026 · 8 min read
Diesen Artikel auf Deutsch lesen

In short

Grömitz pier is 398 metres long and opened in 1984. At the far end sits the diving gondola: €9.00 for adults, €6.00 for children up to 15, €7.00 with a disability card, family packages €21–25. Up to 30 people descend at a time; it opens Wednesday to Saturday, 10am–6pm or 10am–8pm depending on the season.

That makes it one of the longer piers on the Schleswig-Holstein Baltic coast – and unlike most, it is not simply a walkway but a small leisure deck over the water.

What is on the pier?

Halfway along, the pier widens into a sun deck with swivel chairs you can turn into or away from the wind. It sounds like a detail; on a gusty June afternoon it is the difference between staying and leaving. For children there are climbing nets and a lighthouse slide.

Looking back at the shore you get the whole bay: the marina to the left, the beach running north to Lensterstrand on the right, the low cliffs behind. On a clear day you can make out the coast as far as Travemünde.

The diving gondola: prices and opening hours

At the head of the pier sits the Tauchgondel, in operation since September 2009. It is an enclosed cabin lowered slowly beneath the surface. You stay dry, need no equipment and no experience whatsoever.

Check before you walk outHours and prices can change, and the gondola stays shut in strong wind or swell. A quick look at tauchgondel.de or a call to +49 4562 225130 saves the trip.

What is it actually like down there?

Honest answer: the Baltic is not a coral reef. What you see depends heavily on visibility, which ranges from about a metre to several. After days of onshore wind the water is murkier; after a settled high-pressure spell it is markedly clearer. Expect shoals of small fish, starfish, mussel beds, crabs and jellyfish – spring and autumn are livelier than midsummer.

The whole thing takes around half an hour including the commentary. Children almost always love it; adults tend to be surprised by how quiet it is beneath the surface. It also works as a rainy-day plan – just not in a gale.

The best time of day for the pier

  • Early morning: before 8am you have it almost to yourself, with low, soft light.
  • Late afternoon to sunset: the best of it. The bay opens to the south and south-west, so in summer the sun sets over the water – visible from the pier with nothing in the way.
  • After dark: the illuminated pier seen from the promenade is the classic Grömitz picture.

It is busiest between 2pm and 5pm, especially on rainy days during the German school holidays.

Events on and around the pier

The pier is the stage for much of the Grömitz events calendar. Beats über dem Meer and Sunset Chill bring live music out over the water in summer, and Ostsee in Flammen – the big fireworks night on a Saturday in August – is traditionally fired from the pier. Practicalities: getting there, access, weather

From our apartment at Seestraße 9a it is about a ten-minute walk along the promenade. The pier itself is level and step-free, so it works with a wheelchair, walking frame or pushchair – more in our guide to accessible travel in Grömitz.

The pier closes in storm warnings. And one thing first-time visitors underestimate: 398 metres out over open water it is noticeably cooler and windier than on the beach. Bring a jacket, even in July.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the pier in Grömitz?

Grömitz pier is 398 metres long and opened in 1984. Access is free all year round.

What does the diving gondola in Grömitz cost?

Adults pay €9.00, children up to 15 pay €6.00 and visitors with a disability card pay €7.00. Family packages run from €21.00 to €25.00 depending on the number of children (as of August 2026).

When is the Grömitz diving gondola open?

Wednesday to Saturday: 10:00–18:00 in April, May, September and October; 10:00–20:00 from June to August; 11:00–16:00 from November to March. It stays closed in strong wind.

How long does a descent take?

About 30 minutes including the commentary. Up to 30 people go down at once and it is suitable from around age 3.

What can you see in the diving gondola?

Depending on visibility: shoals of small fish, starfish, mussel beds, crabs and jellyfish. The water is much clearer after a settled high-pressure spell than after days of onshore wind.

Is Grömitz pier wheelchair accessible?

Yes. The pier is level and step-free, so it can be used with a wheelchair, walking frame or pushchair. It closes during storm warnings.
